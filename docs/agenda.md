# IMS Data Analysis and Data Storytelling Training

## Overview

- **Format:** One in-person day, 10:00–17:00, delivered in Romanian.
- **Audience:** Beginner and intermediate journalists who are comfortable with basic Excel or Google Sheets. No coding is required.
- **Working method:** Short demonstrations followed by individual work on public, Moldovan data. Participants should bring laptops.
- **Core outcome:** Each participant leaves with a documented data lead, a cleaned working table, a provisional claim, and a draft Datawrapper chart that supports a specific reporting question.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the day, participants will be able to:

1. Locate a relevant public dataset and record who published it, what it covers, when it was updated, and its limits.
2. Make a raw spreadsheet usable for reporting: preserve the original, standardise fields, identify missing or duplicate records, and filter or summarise the data.
3. Turn a reporting question into a careful comparison, check the calculation, and identify what reporting is still needed before publication.
4. Create and annotate a clear, mobile-friendly chart in Datawrapper, including source and methodology notes.
5. Use AI as a limited assistant for public-data cleaning and explanation without treating it as a source or uploading unpublished or personal data.

## Detailed agenda

### 10:00–10:20 — Welcome and working question

**Purpose:** Set a realistic outcome for the day and establish participants' starting points.

1. Welcome, schedule, and working agreements (5 minutes).
2. Show two short examples from Moldova or Eastern Europe and elsewhere: one useful chart and one misleading or incomplete claim (5 minutes).
3. Participants write one issue they cover and one answerable question they would like data to help investigate (10 minutes).

**Facilitator note:** Group participants by similar experience where possible. Collect questions to use in examples later in the day.

### 10:20–11:10 — Finding and verifying public data

**Purpose:** Start with a reporting question rather than a portal tour, and make source verification part of data collection.

1. Translate a question into the data needed: unit of analysis, geography, period, and comparison (5 minutes).
2. Demonstrate a focused search in Moldova's Open Data Portal, the National Bureau of Statistics' StatBank, and MTender open data (10 minutes).
3. Work through a short, pre-checked online-claim verification case: find the earliest available source; check author, date, place, and original context; seek corroboration; and preserve the link, screenshot, and retrieval date (10 minutes).
4. Individual source hunt: find and download, or identify, one dataset relevant to the shared practice question or the participant's beat (15 minutes).
5. Complete a source card: publisher, link, retrieval date, coverage, units, definitions, update date, likely gaps, and a question for the publisher (10 minutes).

**Facilitator note:** Use a known, non-sensitive case for the verification demonstration. It introduces a repeatable workflow; it is not a substitute for a full OSINT investigation. Participants who cannot access a live source use the prepared exercise dataset.

**Output:** One source card and a saved data file or stable source link.

### 11:20–12:30 — Cleaning and structuring data

**Purpose:** Make a small, real dataset ready for exploration without losing the original evidence.

1. Explain the data trail: raw file, working copy, and short data log (10 minutes).
2. Live demonstration in Google Sheets, Excel, or LibreOffice: inspect column types; normalise dates, numbers, and labels; find blank values and duplicates; and retain identifiers (15 minutes).
3. Guided individual exercise using a prepared, locally relevant dataset: clean a small set of records, filter it, and create a simple summary or pivot table (30 minutes).
4. Optional, tool-agnostic AI check: ask for an explanation of a formula or a proposed cleaning rule, then test the answer against the spreadsheet and source documentation. Participants not using an AI tool use the same time for an additional manual quality check. Do not upload confidential, unpublished, or personal data (15 minutes).

**Output:** A cleaned working sheet, a raw-file copy, and a data log stating the changes made.

### 13:30–14:25 — Analysis, interpretation, and verification

**Purpose:** Move from a number to a reportable finding without overstating what the data shows.

1. Work from a hypothesis: what changed, for whom, where, and compared with what? (10 minutes).
2. Explore the cleaned dataset using filters, groupings, pivot tables, and simple rates or percentage changes (20 minutes).
3. Test the result: check totals against the source, inspect outliers and missingness, use an appropriate denominator, and compare the finding with a second source or expert question (15 minutes).
4. Write a provisional claim and a reporting to-do list: documents to request, people to contact, and caveats to disclose (10 minutes).

**Output:** One evidence-backed story lead, its calculation, and its verification/reporting plan.

### 14:25–15:15 — Data visualisation with Datawrapper

**Purpose:** Turn the strongest finding into an honest, understandable chart.

1. Choose a chart based on the question: change over time, comparison, composition, distribution, or location (10 minutes).
2. Demonstrate the Datawrapper workflow: import a clean table, select a chart, format values, and publish a draft (10 minutes).
3. Participants build one chart from their prepared data or the supplied exercise data (20 minutes).
4. Improve it with a claim-led title, meaningful labels, restrained colour, annotations, source and retrieval date, a methods note, and a mobile/accessibility check (10 minutes).

**Output:** A chart link or export that a newsroom could review, not yet publish without editorial and reporting checks.

### 15:25–16:25 — Story sprint

**Purpose:** Connect the data work to a viable journalistic story.

1. Each participant completes an individual one-page story card while the facilitator offers short clinics (30 minutes):
   - reporting question and public-interest rationale;
   - strongest provisional finding and calculation;
   - source, coverage, and key limitation;
   - chart link or sketch;
   - additional documents, interviews, or verification needed; and
   - working headline and audience takeaway.
2. Participants exchange cards in pairs and use a three-question editorial review: **What is the claim? What supports it? What would you need to verify before publication?** (15 minutes).
3. Participants improve their card, prepare a gallery post, and name the next reporting action (15 minutes).

**Output:** An individual newsroom-ready reporting pitch with a clear next step.

### 16:25–17:00 — Gallery review, feedback, and next steps

1. Participants post their chart and story card for a short gallery review. Select a small number for verbal sharing; use the gallery format so everyone receives feedback (20 minutes).
2. Review the reusable checklist: source, method, calculation, context, visual clarity, and editorial verification (5 minutes).
3. Complete an exit card: one thing learned, one story to pursue, and one obstacle needing follow-up support (5 minutes).
4. Explain the resource pack and follow-up support, then close (5 minutes).

## Evidence of learning

The four artefacts below make the day assessable without treating a draft as a publishable investigation:

| Deliverable | Minimum check |
|---|---|
| Source card | Links to the original publisher and records retrieval date, coverage, definitions, and at least one limitation. |
| Working sheet | Preserves raw data, records cleaning choices, and includes one check for missing, duplicate, or implausible values. |
| Provisional claim | Shows the calculation and comparison, names the denominator where relevant, and identifies a reporting or verification gap. |
| Draft chart | States one clear finding; has readable labels, source and retrieval date, a methods/caveat note, and a mobile/accessibility check. |


## Working data plan and source shortlist

### Shared practice dataset

The source session lets participants explore their own story ideas. For the cleaning, analysis, and chart workflow, everyone begins with one small, stable, public Moldovan dataset and a shared reporting question. The exercise pack contains:

- the original downloaded file and a read-only copy;
- a source note with publisher, retrieval date, field definitions, geographic and time coverage, and known limitations;
- a deliberately untidy working copy for the cleaning exercise;
- a facilitator solution and an extension dataset for faster participants; and
- a local/offline backup, so a portal outage or login problem does not consume training time.

Participants may carry their own topic into later analysis after completing the core shared exercise, but only if the source is public, its provenance is clear, and it can be imported without turning the session into technical troubleshooting. The shared dataset remains the fallback that guarantees everyone can complete the analysis and chart exercise.

A current public-procurement, budget, demographic, or environmental dataset is suitable. The verification mini-case must be public, pre-checked, and non-sensitive; it should teach evidence preservation, not ask participants to assess a live high-risk claim.

### Source shortlist

Use live portals for discovery, but do not make the outcome depend on them working in the room:

- [Moldova Open Data Portal](https://dataset.gov.md/) — discovery of government datasets.
- [StatBank, National Bureau of Statistics](https://statbank.statistica.md/) — detailed statistical tables, downloads, and table APIs.
- [MTender open data](https://mtender.gov.md/en/public/open-data) — procurement data and the linked analytical tool.
- Any proprietary source available to participating newsrooms may be shown briefly, but its access, licence, and terms should be checked in advance.

## Deliberate scope choices

The brief includes data journalism, OSINT, online verification, disinformation, AI, and visual storytelling. In one beginner/intermediate day, the useful core is a complete, repeatable data-to-story workflow. Verification of data and online claims is therefore built into the source and analysis sessions.

The following are intentionally not treated as hands-on outcomes for this day:

| Not included as a practical module | Why |
|---|---|
| Full image/video geolocation, satellite analysis, or social-media verification | These need longer case-based practice and should be a separate OSINT workshop. |
| Bot-network mapping or coordinated-disinformation analysis | The work needs platform-specific access, specialist methods, and enough time to validate false positives. |
| Scraping, APIs, coding, or advanced statistics | They would displace core spreadsheet, verification, and reporting skills promised for beginners. |
| A tour of every portal or every Datawrapper chart type | Participants need one usable workflow and one finished chart, not an overwhelming catalogue of tools. |
| AI-generated findings or uploading sensitive material to AI tools | AI output must be checked against original data and should not weaken source protection or editorial accountability. |

Participants who need the excluded OSINT skills should receive a separate follow-up module focused on verification cases and evidence preservation.

## Preparation and delivery requirements

### Before the training

- Send a short pre-training survey covering laptop/software access, spreadsheet confidence, beat or story interests, Datawrapper account status, and accessibility needs.
- Ask participants to create and test a Datawrapper account before the day, or arrange a supported account-creation window before the practical session.
- Test the venue Wi-Fi and the three public sources above. Download the raw data, prepared exercise file, solution file, and slides for offline use.
- Prepare all participant materials in Romanian: source card, data-cleaning checklist, claim canvas, chart checklist, and story card.
- Use only public or explicitly approved material in training tools. Do not ask participants to place protected sources, personal data, or unpublished reporting material in cloud or AI services.

### In the room

- Participants need a laptop, current browser, and a spreadsheet application; the room needs stable internet, projector, screen, and power.
- Keep the exercise dataset small enough to clean and analyse within the session. Retain a more advanced extension dataset for faster participants.
- For a larger cohort, arrange a co-facilitator or structured peer support so hands-on questions do not turn the workshop into individual technical support.

## Follow-up and resource pack

After the session, provide slides; the exercise data and source note; completed example files; a Romanian checklist for source, method, and chart review; links to the portals; and selected guides on data verification and Datawrapper.

Use the follow-up support included in the proposal for story clinics, troubleshooting a participant's first data-driven pitch, or review of a draft chart and methodology note. It should not be used to take over reporting or to validate unverified claims.
